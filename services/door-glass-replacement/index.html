Door glass usually breaks for reasons that have nothing to do with the glass. A hydraulic closer thickens as its fluid cools, so an entrance that was adjusted in July starts throwing the door into the frame in January, several hundred times a day. Salt and grit tracked in from the 401, the 412 and the plaza lots along Thickson Road and Consumers Drive grind through gaskets and setting blocks and eat the finish off aluminum. Freeze-thaw movement works the whole assembly loose, the door drops a fraction, and the lite that has been taking the load finally lets go.

So we look at what is swinging the door before we put new glass into it. Pivots, hinges, sweep, closer speed and latch, the state of the threshold, whether the frame is still square — all of that gets checked, because a new pane in a door that still slams is a pane you will be buying twice. Entrance doors, patio sliders, sidelites and transoms are re-glazed in the safety glass the position requires, and the door goes back squared and swinging properly rather than just filled in.

FAQs

Door Glass and Entrance Lites — your questions

Does the door have to come off for you to re-glaze it?

Usually not. Most aluminum entrance doors, sidelites and transoms are glazed in place from the stops, so the door stays hung and your entrance keeps working through the job. If a door does have to come off — a bent frame, a failed pivot, a corner that has pulled apart — we tell you before we start and secure the opening while it is out.

Why did the glass in my door break when nothing hit it?

Look at the closer and the frame first. Cold thickens hydraulic fluid, so a closer that was fine in summer starts slamming the door in winter; at the same time freeze-thaw movement and a salt-packed threshold stop the door sitting square. The lite ends up taking load it was never meant to carry. Replacing the glass without correcting that is a temporary result.

Does patio door and slider glass count as safety glazing?

Yes. Glass in a door, and in a sidelite wide enough to be mistaken for a doorway, has to be tempered or laminated under the Ontario Building Code — sliders included. We put back the correct type for the position rather than the cheapest sheet that fits, and we confirm which one your opening needs before anything is cut.

Can you sort the closer at the same time as the glass?

That is how we prefer to do it. Setting the closing speed and latch action for winter rather than for the day we happen to be there, checking the pivots and the sweep, and clearing the threshold are all part of making the new lite last. If a closer is past adjusting we will tell you plainly instead of leaving you to find out in January.
