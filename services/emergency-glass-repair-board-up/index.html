Common Whitby broken glass emergency scenarios — and what to do right now

The moment something fails, get us on the phone at (905) 555-0147. Until a Whitby tech arrives, here’s exactly what to do — and what not to do:

1 A flash freeze has locked up the entrance and the glass is taking the strain

Whitby's overnight swings do this every winter, and the lake makes it worse. Meltwater and plow slush run into the glazing pocket and the threshold through a mild afternoon, the temperature falls back through zero after dark, and by morning the ice has the leaf sitting hard on its stops with every drain hole plugged. Add a door closer whose fluid has thickened in the cold, so the arm no longer governs the last part of the swing and the door slams rather than closes, and the tempered lite is absorbing the whole assembly's bad behaviour. Entrances with nothing between them and Lake Ontario go first - Port Whitby, Whitby Shores, the harbour and marina, the GO station - along with the plaza doors off Thickson Road and Consumers Drive that get pulled a few hundred times a day.

Do this right now: Do not put your shoulder into a frozen door. The pane is the softest thing in that assembly and it is what gives way, usually while somebody is standing right in front of it. Keep scrapers, screwdrivers and pry bars away from the glass line and the gaskets, because one nicked edge on a tempered lite is a shatter waiting for the next cold night, and never pour hot water down a frozen pane - the temperature difference will split it where it stands. Clear the ice at the threshold and the bottom rail instead, take the entrance out of service if the lite is already cracked, and route people to another door. When you ring (905) 555-0147, say it is a door rather than a window, because we bring different parts for one than the other.

2 A frontage put through overnight at the four corners or on Baldwin Street

Downtown Whitby trades off its glass. The retail bays around Dundas and Brock are shallow, old and glazed right at sidewalk level, part of that frontage sitting inside the Werden's Plan Heritage Conservation District, and Brooklin is the same problem in a smaller frame - a Heritage Conservation District since 2008, with half of Baldwin Street working out of converted Victorian houses on putty-glazed wood sash. When one of those goes in overnight you have three problems stacked before you have a glass problem: a building anybody can walk into, a public sidewalk with glass across it, and a frame still holding pieces that are standing up under their own weight and nothing else.

Do this right now: Control the area before you look at the damage. Rope it off or park something across the frontage so nobody crosses the drop line, and keep the public back on the sidewalk side, because glass held in a stop releases with no noise to warn anyone. Photograph everything before a broom comes out, because the constable taking your report and the insurance adjuster will both want to see it undisturbed, and closing the opening afterwards does not weaken your claim. Leave the pieces wedged in the frame exactly where they are, and on a heritage sash resist levering off the old stops or putty to speed things along, because that timber is the one component nobody can order in. Ring (905) 555-0147 with the address and whether the unit is standing open to the street.

3 A crack that opened on its own and is now working in the frame

Nothing hit it. On a bright minus-fifteen morning the centre of a south-facing pane takes low winter sun while the edges sit in a frame that has not been above freezing for days, and ordinary annealed glass tears itself apart along that gradient. You can separate it from an impact by its shape: one wandering line that begins at an edge, with no star and no point of contact. What decides whether it is tonight's call or Thursday's is what the crack has done since it appeared. Once it has travelled out to the stops the lite is two pieces being held together by the frame, and on the exposed lots down toward the lake a big pane flexing in an onshore wind finishes the job on its own timetable.

Do this right now: Leave it alone, physically and thermally. Tape is not structural; all it really achieves is persuading somebody it is safe to lean on a pane that has already failed. Pointing a heat gun, hair dryer or kettle at the split just repeats the temperature difference that caused it. Switch off any display light, patio heater or vent blowing across that pane, clear the floor and the ground beneath it, and stop using a door hung in the same frame so nothing slams the glass again. Then describe it to us on the phone: how long the crack is, whether it has reached an edge or the stops, and whether the pane sits above a sidewalk, in a door, or in a room children use. That is what tells us whether you need somebody tonight or on the next working day, and we will give you the honest answer either way.

4 The vision panel is gone from a door that is a fire separation

South Whitby's industrial pocket below the 401 - McQuay Boulevard, Hopkins Street, the units and multi-tenant corporate blocks along Consumers Drive - runs on rated assemblies: stairwell doors, corridor doors between tenants, service doors behind the plazas. The shared corridors in the newer stacked townhouse and mid-rise blocks are the same. A dolly, a pallet or an overnight pry takes the rated vision panel out of one of those doors, and in that moment the door has stopped being a fire separation. Often the same impact leaves it hanging badly enough that it no longer swings shut and latches by itself either. The label etched into the corner of the original pane is usually the only surviving record of what belongs back in that opening.

Do this right now: Treat this as a code compliance matter, not a maintenance ticket. Under Ontario's Fire Code a closure in a fire separation has to be kept in working order, so nothing gets propped, wedged, chained or blocked as a workaround. Do not have window glass, plexiglass or plywood cut in to cover the hole either. What a labelled door is entitled to is fire-protection-rated glazing carrying its own label and cut to that opening; substitute anything else and you have manufactured the defect an inspector is hunting for. Tell whoever holds the building's fire safety plan today, and while the building is occupied station someone at the door if it will not latch on its own. Call (905) 555-0147 and read us whatever is on the label at the door edge if you can reach it - we will make the opening secure now and come back with glazing that carries its own rating.

Emergency FAQs

Broken glass emergency — fast answers

It is well below freezing tonight. Can glass even be installed in this weather, or are we boarded up until spring?

Boarded up until spring is not how this goes. Make-safe happens whatever the thermometer says: we clear the frame and close the opening the same night. It is the permanent glazing the cold actually limits, because sealants, glazing tapes and structural silicones have minimum application temperatures, and a rebate packed with ice will not take a setting block or hold a bead. So we dry and warm the opening, work with products rated for cold-weather application where the job allows, and if a particular wet-sealed unit genuinely cannot be done properly in the conditions we say so rather than fitting something that lets go in April.

Our Whitby storefront was put through overnight. Can we still open in the morning?

Nearly always, and that is the whole point of the board-up. On that first visit we empty the rebate and the stops of standing glass, clear the sidewalk and the parking side so nobody is cut on their way in, and shut the opening behind board or a temporary lite so the unit is secure and weather-tight. We take the measurement before we pull away, which means your replacement pane is being cut while you are serving customers. Oversized, tempered and laminated lites need fabrication time, and you get the realistic wait at the board-up rather than a week later.

The glass is out of a door and now the door will not latch. Is that different from a broken window?

Very different, if that door forms part of a fire separation - which in Whitby's multi-tenant industrial and corporate units below the 401 it very often does. Ontario's Fire Code requires a closure in a fire separation to be kept in working order, so a door that no longer swings shut and latches is a compliance problem and the person holding your fire safety plan needs to hear about it today. Nothing gets wedged or chained as a stopgap in the meantime. It also settles what is allowed back into the opening, which is fire-protection-rated glazing with a label on it - not ordinary window glass, and not plastic.

Half the storefronts on Baldwin Street in Brooklin are converted Victorian houses. Can you board and re-glaze a wood sash?

Yes, and the timber is exactly why we are careful at the board-up rather than apologetic afterwards. Brooklin's downtown core has carried Heritage Conservation District designation since 2008, and those frontages are original wood frames with putty glazing and pane sizes no supplier holds. Where the profile allows it we fix the board to the surround instead of driving fastenings through the sash, we keep sound original stops, and the replacement is cut to the sightline we measure off the opening itself. If the reglazing changes the exterior appearance enough to want the Town consulted, you hear that before anything is cut.

How fast can somebody be here, and do you come out to Brooklin and Ashburn at night?

The north end is a normal part of the run - Brooklin, Ashburn, Myrtle and the rural roads above Winchester, the same as downtown, Port Whitby and the plazas along Thickson and Taunton. What you will not get from us is a stopwatch promise on arrival, because it hangs on where the truck happens to be when you ring and on what else in Durham is standing open that night. Tell dispatch your cross-streets and whether the building is open to the street, and you will get a straight answer about who is nearest and when they can realistically reach you.
