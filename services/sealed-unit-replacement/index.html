The haze between the panes is not dirt and it will not clean off. A double-glazed unit is two sheets of glass bonded around a spacer, and the sealed cavity between them does the insulating. Whitby weather works that seal hard: the air inside expands through a humid August off the lake and contracts hard in a January deep freeze, and that annual pumping eventually breaks the perimeter seal down. Once it goes, moisture gets in, the argon fill is gone, and the fog you can see is the visible half of a window that has quietly stopped doing its job.

This is our steadiest residential work in Whitby, and the reason is demographic rather than mysterious. Rolling Acres, Taunton North, Williamsburg and Whitby Shores filled in through the 1990s and 2000s, so an entire cohort of sealed units is reaching the end of its service life within a few years of itself — one fogged pane this spring, three more on the same elevation next spring, and a low winter sun that makes every one of them obvious at once. The fix is not a new window. We measure the unit, confirm the glass make-up, spacer and low-e coating, and swap the insulated unit into the frame you already own. Sash, frame, hardware and finish stay exactly where they are.

FAQs

Sealed Unit and Fogged Thermal Pane Replacement — your questions

Every window on one side of my house has fogged in the same year — why?

Because they were all installed in the same year. Sealed units have a service life, and in the subdivisions built through the 1990s and 2000s an entire elevation reaches the end of it at roughly the same time. The south and west elevations usually go first, because they take the most thermal cycling. It is not a defect in your house and it is not something you caused.

Can the fog be cleared without replacing the unit?

You will find people who drill and vent the unit to clear the visible haze. It changes how the window looks and nothing else: the seal is not restored, the insulating gas is not coming back, and the cavity stays open to outside air afterwards. If you want the window insulating again rather than just looking better, the unit itself has to be replaced.

Is it worth doing before winter, or can it wait?

It is not an emergency call — nobody needs us at two in the morning for a fogged pane. But a blown unit has lost its insulating value, so the room runs colder, the glass sweats and you pay for the difference every month of the heating season. Doing it in autumn rather than in February is the cheaper order of operations. If the glass has actually cracked through, that is a different conversation.

Will the replacement match the rest of the windows?

That is the point of measuring rather than ordering a nearest-fit. We confirm the glass make-up, the spacer, the thickness and whether the original had a low-e coating or a tint, so the new unit reads the same from the street as the ones either side of it. A mismatched coating is very visible on a full elevation in low sun.
