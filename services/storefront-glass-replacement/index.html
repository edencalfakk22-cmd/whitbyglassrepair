FAQs

Storefront Glass Replacement — your questions

My shopfront went through overnight — what happens between now and the new pane?

We attend, take the hanging glass out of the frame, sweep the sidewalk so nobody gets cut on the way to work, and close the opening with board or temporary glazing so you can trade in the morning. The measurement is taken on that same visit. Oversized, tinted and laminated lites are cut to order, so we tell you the honest lead time while we are boarding rather than a week later.

Will a new pane look wrong next to the rest of my Brooklin or downtown frontage?

Not if it is specified properly. Old glass is rarely the same thickness or tint as modern stock, so we match both against the neighbouring bays instead of ordering the nearest equivalent. On a conservation-district frontage we also keep the original frame profile, stops and bead where they are sound and replace only the putty, rubber and setting blocks that have perished.

Do you have to replace the whole frame, or just the glass?

Almost always just the glass. A century-old storefront frame is usually still doing its job even when the pane in it is in pieces on the floor — what fails is the putty, the gasket and the setting blocks underneath. We make those good and re-glaze into the existing frame. If the frame itself has rotted, corroded or racked far enough that it will not hold a lite safely, we say so before ordering rather than after.

Can you work outside trading hours so I do not lose a day?

Yes, and on a downtown frontage that is usually the sensible way to do it. Board-ups happen whenever the break happens; the glazing itself we schedule around your hours where the pane and the access allow it. Tell us when you open and we will work back from that.

How much does storefront glass replacement cost in Whitby?

Storefront work in Whitby is quoted per opening, because no two bays on Brock Street are the same size, thickness or glass type. What we look at is how big the lite is, whether it has to be laminated or toughened, what the frame needs before it will hold glass again, and whether the opening had to be boarded first. You get one written scope after a free visit, and the figure does not move afterwards.
