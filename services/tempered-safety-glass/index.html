Safety glass is not an upgrade in Ontario, it is a requirement in the places that matter: entrance doors, sidelites wider than 500 mm that could be mistaken for a doorway, low panes, guards, shower enclosures and anywhere someone can walk into glass. Tempered glass breaks into blunt pellets; laminated glass cracks but stays held in its plastic interlayer, which is why it is the right call anywhere falling glass would land on people below — balcony guards on the newer Brock Street mid-rises being the obvious Whitby example.

FAQs

Tempered and Laminated Safety Glass — your questions

What is the difference between tempered and laminated glass?

Tempered glass is heat-treated so it breaks into small blunt pellets instead of shards. Laminated glass is two panes bonded to a plastic interlayer, so when it cracks the pieces stay stuck to the interlayer and the opening stays covered. Guards and overhead glazing usually call for laminated for exactly that reason.

Can tempered glass be cut down on site?

No. Once glass is tempered it cannot be cut, drilled or edge-worked — any attempt shatters the whole pane. Every dimension, hole and notch has to be right before fabrication, which is why we measure your Whitby opening on site ourselves rather than quoting from a phone measurement.

Which openings in my Whitby property legally need safety glazing?

Glass in a door, in a sidelite over 500 mm wide that could be mistaken for a doorway, in low panes near floor level, in guards and in shower and tub enclosures all has to be safety glazing to CAN/CGSB-12.1-M under the Ontario Building Code. We assess the specific opening and install the correct type rather than copying whatever happened to be in there before.
