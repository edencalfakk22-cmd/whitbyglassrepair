Built for Whitby

Whitby properties, Whitby expertise

  • 🔧

    Storefront glass at the four corners and along Brock Street

    Downtown Whitby carries more than 450 businesses around the four corners at Dundas and Brock, and much of that frontage is old: shallow retail bays in buildings that predate anyone's memory of them, some inside the Werden's Plan Heritage Conservation District, with big single-glazed lites sitting at sidewalk level in frames that have been moving for a century. Delivery dollies, patio furniture, snow shovels and buildings that are no longer square all take their turn. We measure the opening as it sits today rather than trusting an original drawing, and every entrance lite goes back in tempered or laminated safety glass.

  • 🔧

    Brooklin's Baldwin Street heritage frontages

    Downtown Brooklin has been a designated Heritage Conservation District under the Ontario Heritage Act since 2008, and the village dates to 1840. Half the businesses on Baldwin Street trade out of converted Victorian houses, which means wood stops, putty-glazed sash and pane sizes that no supplier holds in stock. That work is measured to the millimetre and matched for thickness and tint, because on a heritage street the whole block reads as one frontage and a mismatched lite is obvious from across the road.

  • 🔧

    A fast-growing town with a lot of residential glass coming due

    Whitby was home to 138,501 people at the 2021 census and is one of the faster-growing communities in the country, with Durham Region projected to roughly double toward 1.3 million residents by 2051. The practical consequence for us is simple: the subdivisions that filled Rolling Acres, Taunton North, Williamsburg and Whitby Shores through the 1990s and 2000s are now hitting the age where sealed units start failing in numbers, while new mid-rise and mixed-use builds along Brock Street add ground-floor commercial glass every year. Steady residential work through the week, storefront work when something goes through a window.

  • 🔧

    Thermal stress cracks on cold, bright Whitby mornings

    From November through March the middle of a south-facing pane bakes in low winter sun while its edges stay buried in a frame sitting at minus ten or colder. That difference is enough to split ordinary annealed glass, which is why so many Whitby cracks appear overnight with nothing having touched the window. A thermal crack starts at the edge and wanders as a single line, and it keeps travelling with every freeze-thaw swing — so it never stops on its own, and our winters hand it plenty of swings.

  • 🔧

    Lake Ontario wind, snow squalls and flash freezes at the waterfront

    Port Whitby, Whitby Shores and the harbour and marina sit fully exposed to whatever comes off Lake Ontario. Squall days drive wet snow and grit horizontally into ground-floor glazing, a flash freeze locks it into every gasket and drain hole overnight, and the next thaw lifts water back into the glazing rebate. Wind-borne debris around the GO station and the waterfront trail does the rest. Down there we check drainage and setting blocks as a matter of course, because a lakefront opening that cannot shed water eats sealed units.

  • 🔧

    Road salt, grit and cold-stiffened closers at the entrance

    Salt and grit tracked in from Highway 401, the 412 and the plaza lots along Thickson Road and Consumers Drive chew through gaskets, setting blocks and the finish on aluminum frames, while freeze-thaw movement works the whole assembly loose. Add a hydraulic door closer that thickens as the fluid cools in January and starts slamming the door several hundred times a day, and door glass cracks for reasons that have nothing to do with the glass. We check the frame, stops, pivots and swing before we re-glaze so the new pane is not simply the next one to break.

Free Whitby estimates

What goes into your estimate

We do not price glass & glazing off a list — we look, then we scope. What tends to decide where a Whitby estimate lands:

  • Timing — A scheduled measure-and-install vs. an overnight board-up and rush cut.
  • Safety-glazing rules — Glass in a door, beside a door, or low enough to walk into has to be tempered or laminated.
  • Stock vs. custom cut — Common sizes come off the shelf; oversized, shaped or coated units are fabricated to order.
  • Height & glazing method — A ground-floor swap vs. an upper lite that has to be re-taped or wet-sealed off a lift.

Whitby FAQs

Your questions, answered

Broken tonight — what actually happens before morning?

We get to the opening, take out whatever glass is still hanging in the frame, sweep the sidewalk and the entrance, and close it with board or a temporary glazed panel so the building is secure and you can trade. Then we measure. The permanent pane follows the fabrication: stock sizes are quick, toughened, laminated and sealed units are made to your measurement, and you get that date on the night.

There is no glazing licence in Ontario — so what should we judge you on?

On the things that can actually be checked. A current liability insurance certificate and WSIB clearance, both of which we hand over before starting. A workmanship guarantee in writing. And the specification itself: whether the person quoting can tell you which of your openings the code requires safety glazing in, and whether they will put annealed glass back into one that does. We will not.

It was a break-in — how do we handle the insurance side?

Photograph everything before anybody sweeps, including the point of entry and the glass on the ground, because the insurer and the police report both want it and it cannot be recreated later. From there we provide an itemized scope and invoicing an adjuster can work with, and we can bill the insurer directly where the policy allows. Getting the opening secured does not prejudice a claim.

Half the windows on one side of the house have hazed over — why now?

Because they all went in at the same time. Rolling Acres, Taunton North, Williamsburg and Whitby Shores filled in through the 1990s and 2000s, so a whole cohort of sealed units is reaching the end of its life within a few years of itself — and the south and west elevations, which take the most thermal cycling, generally go first. Low winter sun then makes every one of them obvious on the same morning.

Do you cover Brooklin as well as Whitby?

Yes. Brooklin, Ashburn and the rural north end are part of Whitby and part of our regular run, and we know what Baldwin Street's heritage frontages need — measured sash, matched thickness, and respect for the conservation district. Call us and we will confirm scheduling for your street.

Which parts of Whitby do you cover?

All of it — downtown Whitby, Werden's Plan, Port Whitby, Whitby Shores, Pringle Creek, Rolling Acres, Williamsburg, Taunton North, Blue Grass Meadows, Lynde Creek, West Lynde and Brooklin — plus Oshawa, Ajax and the surrounding Durham Region and Greater Toronto Area on request.
